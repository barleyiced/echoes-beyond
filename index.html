DU Companion

What this is

A second opinion for a Divergent Universe: Arcadian Chronicles run. Tell it where you are and what you hold, and it scores what the game offers against the datamined numbers: Blessings, Curios, doors, Occurrence options, Workbench spending, Masks. You see why one pick beats another.

Start on this tab. Set your Domain x / y and difficulty in the bar above, pick your Mask, add your team. Then use Decide when the game offers you something, Door at a waypoint, Spend at a shop or Occurrence.

This is a heuristic, not a solved game. The weights are informed guesses that nobody has checked against a real Difficulty 5 clear. Every score shows its working on How it scores, so overrule it when you know better. Your browser keeps your run and sends it nowhere. Use Export on What I own if you want a copy you can keep.

Wishpower level-up

Every Wishpower level offers a hand of Miracles. Almost all of them are called "Ordinary", "Rare" or "Extraordinary Miracle", so the name tells you nothing. Pick them out of your Mask's own pool by what they do. Reshuffling the hand ranks alongside them, priced against the pool it draws from.

Which Domain to designate?

Nearly a third of Miracles hand you a second choice. "Designate 1 Domain to become a Blank", "attach the beacon to a designated Domain". That choice often matters more than the Miracle did. A sacrifice should land on your least valuable card, an investment on your best, and the ranking flips accordingly.

What a score is, and what it is not

This tool has no damage model and does not pretend to one. It reads the game's own datamined numbers, tags what each card does, and adds up a handful of weighted factors. That is the whole trick.

  • Scores are relative, never absolute. 87 against 61 means "clearly better among these three cards". It does not mean 87% optimal, and two scores from different screens are not comparable.
  • Every verdict shows its working. Each card lists its factors, the raw 0 to 1 value of each, the weight, and the points. When you disagree with a recommendation, you disagree with one specific line, and that line is the one to change.
  • The engine reads effect text, not names. Card names in this mode carry almost no information: 136 of the 286 Wishpower Miracles are literally called "Ordinary Miracle" or "Rare Miracle". Everything is scored from what the card says it does.
  • Anything it could not read, it says it could not read. A card with too little machine-readable text carries a low-confidence warning instead of a confident-looking number.

The rules the whole thing obeys

These are design invariants, not preferences. A test pins each one, because each one went wrong at some point and nothing showed it.

  1. Never confidently wrong. "I'm unsure, pick one" is fine. A confident verdict that is wrong is not, because it corrupts the run state and every recommendation after it.
  2. Skip is a ranked competitor, never a fallback. If nothing beats doing nothing, the answer is do nothing. Screens with no "take nothing" button still have one: reshuffle at a Wishpower level-up, redraw at a waypoint.
  3. Rarity never justifies a spend. Value is marginal gain × relevance. A Legendary on an abandoned Path loses to a Rare on the Equation you are chasing.
  4. Every verdict explains itself. You cannot overrule a recommendation that states no reason, which defeats the point of it.
  5. Concentration beats breadth. Equation credit is the best track a pick advances, not the sum over all of them. Summing rewarded exactly the dilution this tool exists to prevent.
  6. Scans fill in, they never apply themselves. A partial inventory scan never deletes anything.
  7. Domains remaining drives everything positional, never the Plane number. The same Domain index means different things in a 13- and a 20-Domain run.
  8. The build refuses to ship a dataset that fails verification. Silent wrongness is the failure mode the whole tool exists to avoid.

How sure is any of this

The honest answer differs sharply by section, so each block above is labelled.

data Read out of the pinned game files. Facts, held at one exact upstream commit so recommendations cannot change silently under you.
measured Computed from that data, or observed on a real run and reported. Not a guess, but not something the game states either.
estimate A guess. The shape is defensible. A consumable is worth less than a permanent, and deck effects decay as the run runs out. Nobody has measured the magnitudes.

Most of what turns into a recommendation sits in that third category, and a tidy table that implied otherwise would be dishonest. Nobody has validated: every weight on this page, the 1.4 blessing-picks-per-Domain estimate, the store floor and its multipliers, the Heat costs (players reported those, since no cost table exists anywhere in the game files), the Legendary store price, beacon values, Miracle and Mask weights, and the reference run the rating compares against.

All of it lives in engine/weights.yaml and the constants named beside each block above. Nothing hides in code. To fix a number, run alongside a real clear, note every call you disagreed with, and change whatever produced it.
